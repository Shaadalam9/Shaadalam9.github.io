About me

Full bio, journey & awards →

I study how pedestrians and other road users behave in real-world traffic, using computer vision, deep learning and reinforcement learning on large-scale video from around the world. My PhD is in the Department of Industrial Design at TU Eindhoven, and from January 2027 I will join KPMG Netherlands as an AI Engineer, bringing these methods from research into real-world products.

Before that I did an MSc in Robotics and AI at IIT Madras, training deep reinforcement learning controllers for autonomous ships.

What I work on

All research →

Pedestrians across the planet

Thousands of hours of YouTube dashcam footage from {{ site.data.crowd.countries }} countries and territories show how people cross streets around the world, well beyond WEIRD samples.

Virtual reality experiments

Pedestrians stand at a virtual kerb as automated cars approach, so we can safely test how yielding, eHMIs, vehicle sounds and other pedestrians change when people feel safe to cross.

AI in place of participants?

Can vision-language models such as GPT-4V predict how people judge risk around automated vehicles and their external displays (eHMIs)?

Generative AI for traffic scenes

Generating realistic traffic scenarios and changing light and weather in traffic video with GANs and video models such as Veo 3.

Deep RL for autonomous ships

Fast-tracking deep reinforcement learning controllers for vessels by first learning from trusted PID and line-of-sight autopilots.

CROWD: City Road Observations With Dashcams

How do pedestrians cross the road in Lagos, Hanoi or Eindhoven? A major part of my PhD is CROWD, a global dataset of dashcam and walking footage collected from YouTube and analysed with computer vision to compare road user behaviour across countries and cultures.
